Four Points is the floor game. It wants a landscape picture, lying on its side, so the camera can see you from your hands to your feet.

Four Points

Brace on your hands and feet. The structure above you groans before it loads, and you choose how to hold it.

How it goes

A sound tells you which way the weight is coming. You have a couple of seconds to get into the configuration that carries it, and you have to be there and still when the beat lands. Then the load resolves against the shape you are actually in.

It is a duel of turns, not a test of reflexes. Nothing asks you to react to something that has already arrived.

Before anything else

Put the phone on the floor on its side, a couple of metres away, leaning on something so it looks slightly down at you. It has to see the whole of you, hands to feet.

The camera has never been pointed at a body on the floor before this build. The setup below measures whether it can see you properly and tells you straight if it cannot.

Round one

Three ways to brace, three kinds of load, fourteen exchanges. Each way carries exactly one load and fails the other two, so there is no posture to hide in.

Later rounds use all six, where some postures partly carry a load instead of failing it outright.

Finding the floor

The game has no idea which way up the room is. It works that out from your own hands and feet, by watching you hold a few shapes. Everything it scores afterwards is measured against that line, so this part matters more than it looks.

 

Skipping is allowed and always will be. The check is advice, not a lock — but a floor line more than about seven degrees out costs a clean hold, measured, so a skipped check means scores you should not trust.

The full table

Round one names the posture that answers each load while you learn it. After that it only names the load, and when something gives way it tells you what would have held — so the table gets learned by playing it. This is the whole thing, for studying.
