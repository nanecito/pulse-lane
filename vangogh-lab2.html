You march to drive and steer with your hands. Four rivals race you over one lap, and the nearest one sits on your tail while you are working. The road and the numbers stay plain so you can read them; the sky and the fields carry the brushwork. Paintings line the circuit, a different six every race, and at the finish you are asked who painted one of them.

MarchStep on the spot, or dance to the beat — any steady rhythm counts, however small. Faster steps mean a faster car. Ease off and you lose speed, and whoever is behind you will come past.
SteerHands at chest height, elbows bent, as if holding a wheel. Turn it to turn the car.
SquatSquat to brake. The deeper you go, the harder it brakes. Nothing else slows you — your hands only steer.
JumpOnly if you run off the road: jump, with both feet, to haul yourself back onto it. On the track it does nothing.
CornersChevrons and a coloured road warn you well ahead, and an arrow on screen shows which way it goes. Brake early, lean through it, and you get a burst of speed out the other side.
